## Part A — 2025 sample midterm, worked

### Vitamin D3 + calcium cancer trial (JAMA 2017)

**Double-blind.** *Definition:* neither the participants nor the investigators/staff who give the pills and assess outcomes know who is in which arm. *Purpose:* prevent information bias — placebo effects, different behavior, and biased reporting or outcome assessment between arms.

**Placebo-controlled.** *Definition:* the comparison arm gets an inert pill identical in appearance to the active supplement. *Purpose:* makes blinding possible, separates the true effect from the placebo effect, and gives a fair comparison of side effects.

**Population-based.** *Definition:* participants were recruited from the general population of a defined area (31 rural counties), not from clinics or self-referred volunteers. *Purpose:* improves external validity — results generalize better to the target population.

**Randomized.** *Definition:* assignment to arm by chance, with each person having a known, predetermined probability. *Purpose:* the arms are comparable on known and unknown risk factors, so differences in outcome can be attributed to the intervention (protects internal validity; prevents selection bias/confounding).

**Censored — what it means.** We stop observing the person before they've had the outcome; all we know is that they were cancer-free up to their last observation.

**Reasons for censoring in any trial.** Lost to follow-up (moved, couldn't be contacted), withdrew consent/changed mind, died of another cause, or the study ended before they had the event (administrative censoring).

**2,843 excluded of 5,146 assessed — internal or external validity?** External. The exclusions (ineligibility, 2,040 declining) happened *before* randomization, so they don't make the two arms less comparable; they change *who* the results apply to. Women who agree to a 4-year supplement trial may be healthier or more health-conscious than the target population.

**Intent-to-treat numbers.** Everyone randomized: **1,156** (vitamin D + Ca) and **1,147** (placebo). (The paper's "primary analysis" used 1,102 and 1,095 — a modified ITT that dropped the 54 and 52 who withdrew with no follow-up data at all, because there's no outcome information to analyze for them.)

**Per-protocol numbers** (the question says several answers are acceptable). Subtracting everyone who withdrew or discontinued: vitamin D + Ca = 1,156 − 54 − 73 − 238 = **791**; placebo = 1,147 − 52 − 60 − 246 = **789**. An alternate defensible answer subtracts only those who discontinued the intervention from the primary-analysis group: 1,102 − 238 = **864** and 1,095 − 246 = **849**.

**Calculation method for the figure.** Kaplan-Meier (plotted as cumulative incidence = 1 − KM survival), compared with the log-rank test.

**Median survival in each group.** **Not reached / cannot be estimated** in either group — only ~4–6% developed cancer, far below the 50% needed for a median.

**Null hypothesis of the log-rank test.** The cancer-free survival curves of the two groups are the same over the whole follow-up period.

**Cumulative probability of being cancer-free at 4 years, vitamin D + Ca.** About 1 − 0.042 ≈ **0.96 (≈ 96%)**.

**Conclusion.** No statistically significant evidence that vitamin D + calcium prevents cancer: log-rank p = 0.06 > 0.05, so we fail to reject H0. The curves trend toward benefit, so the trial may have been underpowered — "not significant" isn't proof of no effect, but it doesn't support claiming prevention.

### Melanoma: Madison vs Orlando

**Age-specific rates.** (a) Madison 55–64: 100 ÷ 72,000 × 100,000 = **138.9 per 100,000**. (b) Orlando 25–34: 38 ÷ 146,000 × 100,000 = **26.0 per 100,000**.

**Crude rates.** Madison: 518 ÷ 647,000 × 100,000 = **80.1 per 100,000 per year**. Orlando: 1,242 ÷ 734,600 × 100,000 = **169.1 per 100,000 per year**.

**Expected cases** (rate × standard population ÷ 100,000):

| Age | Madison rate | Madison expected | Orlando rate | Orlando expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15–24 | 0.6 | 2.1 | 2.2 | 8 |
| 25–34 | 13.0 | 35 | 26.0 | 70 |
| 35–44 | 31.3 | 68 | 99.2 | 216 |
| 45–54 | 77.2 | 157 | 198.2 | 402 |
| 55–64 | 138.9 | **215** | 313.3 | 486 |
| 65–74 | 240.7 | **267** | 553.6 | 614 |
| 75–84 | 406.3 | **248** | 775.9 | 473 |
| 85+ | 500 | **80** | 855.3 | 137 |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1,072** | | **≈ 2,406** |

**Age-adjusted rates.** Madison: 1,072 ÷ 1,387,600 × 100,000 = **77.3 per 100,000**. Orlando: 2,406 ÷ 1,387,600 × 100,000 = **173.4 per 100,000**.

**Same story or different?** Same story: Orlando's rate is about twice Madison's whether crude (169.1 vs 80.1, ratio 2.1) or adjusted (173.4 vs 77.3, ratio 2.2). The two cities have similar age structures (about 26% vs 24% aged 55+), so age wasn't distorting the crude comparison much; the difference reflects something else (e.g., sun exposure). The small shifts make sense: Madison is slightly older than the standard (adjusted < crude), Orlando slightly younger (adjusted > crude).

### Phase III

**(c)** A full-scale clinical trial to determine the efficacy of a drug.

### Definitions

**NNT.** Number needed to treat = 1 ÷ (rate in control group − rate in treated group): how many people must receive the intervention to prevent one bad outcome. *Purpose:* an intuitive measure of absolute benefit that reflects baseline risk — useful for clinical and policy decisions (cost, harms).

**Salk vaccine.** Jonas Salk's injected, inactivated (killed) poliovirus vaccine, tested in the 1954 field trial of ~1.8 million children (observed-control and randomized placebo-controlled parts). *Purpose:* prevent paralytic polio by producing bloodstream antibodies that stop the virus from reaching the nervous system (it does not prevent intestinal infection).

### Cervical cancer: Pap vs HPV

**Sensitivity of Pap.** 55 ÷ 100 = **55%**.

**Specificity of HPV.** 9,306 ÷ 9,900 = **94.0%**.

**Higher PPV?** **Pap**: 55 ÷ 352 = 15.6% vs HPV: 95 ÷ 689 = 13.8% (Pap's higher specificity, 97%, means fewer false positives).

**Prevalence.** 100 ÷ 10,000 = **1%**.

**Better for preventing deaths?** **HPV test** — sensitivity 95% vs 55% means it misses 5 instead of 45 of every 100 cancers. When early treatment greatly reduces mortality, catching cases matters most; its extra false positives can be sorted out with follow-up testing.

## Part B — Fresh practice problems (study guide Section 7)

**P1.** (a) 200/10,000 = **2%**. (b) 150/9,800 at risk = **1.53%** (exclude the 200 prevalent cases from the denominator). (c) 30/10,000 × 100,000 = **300 per 100,000 per year**. (d) 30/(200 + 150) = **8.6%**.

**P2.** Person-years = 2 + 3 + 1.5 + 4 + 0.5 = 11; events = 2. Rate = 2/11 = 0.18 per person-year = **18.2 per 100 person-years**.

**P3.** (a) Crude A = 86/16,000 = **5.38 per 1,000**; crude B = 94/12,000 = **7.83 per 1,000**. (b) A's rates 2, 5, 20 per 1,000 → expected 20 + 50 + 100 = 170 → 170/25,000 = **6.8 per 1,000**. B's rates 1, 4, 15 per 1,000 → expected 10 + 40 + 75 = 125 → **5.0 per 1,000**. (c) **A** has higher mortality — its age-specific rates are higher in every age group. B's crude rate is higher only because B is much older (42% aged 65+ vs 12.5%). Age was a confounder that flipped the comparison.

**P4.** Expected = 4,000 × 0.001 + 1,000 × 0.006 = 4 + 6 = **10**. SMR = 18/10 = **1.8**. Factory workers had 80% more deaths than expected if they had national age-specific death rates.

**P5.** Potato salad: AR 75% vs 15%, **RR 5.0**. Chicken: AR 50% vs 55%, RR 0.91. Vehicle = **potato salad** (high AR in eaters, low in non-eaters, 45 of 51 ill people ate it). Odds of illness among potato-salad eaters = 45/15 = **3.0**.

**P7.** (a) 100 diseased: TP 90, FN 10; 1,900 non-diseased: TN 1,805, FP 95. PPV = 90/185 = **48.6%**; NPV = 1,805/1,815 = **99.4%**. (b) 400 diseased: TP 360, FN 40; 1,600 non-diseased: TN 1,520, FP 80. PPV = 360/440 = **81.8%**; NPV = 1,520/1,560 = **97.4%**. (c) Se and Sp didn't change; higher prevalence raised PPV sharply and lowered NPV slightly, because true positives grow relative to false positives.

**P8.** I_exp = 4%, I_unexp = 1%, I_total = 80/5,000 = 1.6%. **RR = 4.0**; **AR = 3 per 100 (30 per 1,000) over 5 years**; **AR% = 75%**; **PAR = 0.6 per 100** (check: AR × 0.20 exposed = 0.006); **PAR% = 37.5%**. AR%: among smokers, 75% of cases are attributable to smoking. PAR%: in the whole population, 37.5% of cases are attributable to smoking.

**P9.** VE = (2% − 0.4%)/2% = **80%**. NNT = 1/(0.02 − 0.004) = 62.5 → **63** vaccinated to prevent one case.

**P10.** Exposed cases 60, unexposed cases 40; exposed controls 50, unexposed controls 150. OR = (60 × 150)/(50 × 40) = **4.5** — the odds of exposure were 4.5 times higher in cases than controls (equivalently, odds of disease 4.5 times higher in the exposed). **No RR**: the investigator fixed the case-to-control ratio, so incidence can't be estimated.

**P11.** (a) **Confounding** — stratum RRs are similar to each other (1.4, 1.5) but far from the crude (3.0); the adjusted (M-H) RR would fall between the stratum values, around 1.4–1.5. Positive confounding (crude farther from 1). (b) **Interaction / effect modification** — stratum RRs differ from each other (1.0 vs 5.0); report them separately rather than averaging.

**P12.** R0 = 4 → 1 − 1/4 = **75%**. R0 = 12 → **91.7%**.

**P13.** Observed = (50 + 110)/200 = **80%**. Chance both + = 0.30 × 0.40 = 0.12; chance both − = 0.70 × 0.60 = 0.42; chance agreement = **54%**. κ = (0.80 − 0.54)/(1 − 0.54) = **0.57** — moderate agreement beyond chance.

**P14.** RR = 0.20/0.10 = **2.0**. SE(ln RR) = √[(1 − 0.2)/20 + (1 − 0.1)/10] = √0.13 = 0.361. ln 2 = 0.693; 0.693 ± 1.96 × 0.361 → (−0.014, 1.400). Exponentiate: **95% CI (0.99, 4.05)**. It includes 1 → **not statistically significant** at α = 0.05, despite a doubled risk (small numbers of cases → imprecise).
